# Time Signatures Nov 7 2026

**How the reader works now:**

The song reader now takes tempo in beats: **tempo N beats/min or tempo N beats/s**. The meter defines the beat.

- The beat comes from the meter. In simple time it’s the lower figure’s note. In compound time, with 6, 9 or 12 on top, it’s three of those, a dotted note. So in 3/8 at 30 beats/min the bar plays 30 eighths a minute. In 6/8 at 60 beats/min it plays 60 dotted quarters a minute, which is 180 eighths a minute.
- Tempo and meter can come in either order within a section.
- A carried tempo keeps the note speed, as you chose. Say a 4/4 section at 120 beats/min is followed by one that writes only meter 6/8. The quarters stay at 120 a minute, which is 80 dotted-quarter beats a minute.
- Tempos must be whole numbers. 112.5 beats/min is refused for now; decimal tempos are still an open question.
- Old units say what to write instead. q/min, e/min and the like give, for example: e/min is no longer read. Write the tempo in beats, e.g. tempo 30 beats/min. The meter says what a beat is (in 6/8, a dotted quarter).
- 15/8, 18/8 and other multiples of 3 above 12 are refused, with: 18/8 is not read yet: compound meters stop at 12 on top. Write it as shorter bars, e.g. 6/8, 9/8 or 12/8. Other figures above 12, such as 13/8, are read as simple time.
